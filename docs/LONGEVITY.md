# The 100+ year survival strategy

> **The one-paragraph answer.** zelynic does not survive a century
> by being maintained perfectly — it survives by needing less
> maintenance than anything else in its class. The bet is placed on
> the only substrate with a track record longer than any company:
> Linux itself. The design keeps the kernel-facing surface tiny,
> the release one self-contained binary, the dependency count low,
> the economics funded by the people who profit from the tool, and
> every load-bearing decision written down where no single head is
> load-bearing. The strategy is deliberately short — a survival
> plan that needs a long document has already lost.

This page answers the NIGHT-think-like-light-years-4 ask: think in
light years, not quarters — what keeps a network tool alive for a
century? It is strategy, not procedure: the recurring work lives in
[MAINTENANCE.md](MAINTENANCE.md), the design reasoning lives in
[PHILOSOPHY.md](PHILOSOPHY.md), and this page only says why the
whole shape of the project is the survival plan.

## 1. The bet is on the substrate

Software does not survive because people love it; it survives
because it stands on something that survives. The Unix lineage is
the oldest continuously-maintained software family in production
use (1969 → today), and Linux — its most successful descendant,
born 1991 — is the kernel of the internet's servers, every major
cloud, and Android. Companies die, foundations drift, and products
get sunset; a mainline kernel subsystem with thousands of paid
maintainers does not. That is the floor zelynic stands on, and it
is the only floor in this industry with a century-scale record.

Two properties make the bet sound rather than romantic. First,
eBPF has been merged mainline since kernel 3.18 (2014) — it is
maintained in-tree by the kernel community, not an out-of-tree
patch that rots the day its author walks away. Second, cgroup v2 —
the accounting unit the whole tool is keyed on — is the kernel's
own process-container model, not a userspace convention any vendor
can deprecate. The kernel floor is 5.13+ and the verified matrix
climbs from there ([KERNEL_COMPATIBILITY.md](KERNEL_COMPATIBILITY.md));
the kernel version is the contract, and the distro is noise.

The Linux-only refusal is the same strategy wearing different
clothes. Every port doubles the surface, every abstraction layer
buys compatibility at the price of distance from the substrate,
and a tool that runs everywhere must be maintained everywhere or
rots everywhere (see [COSMIC_DRAGON_ARCHITECTURE.md](COSMIC_DRAGON_ARCHITECTURE.md)
for the architecture-side argument). Depth over breadth is not a
limitation here; it is the survival plan stated as a design rule.

## 2. The design already pays rent

Nothing in this section was built "for longevity" — every choice
below was made for correctness, and longevity is what correctness
compounds into when the surface is small:

- **verdict-only, one hooking layer.** the cosmic dragon engine's
  six-layer verdict path ([INNOVATIONS.md](INNOVATIONS.md)) is the
  entire kernel-facing contract. A tiny surface means a tiny
  breakage surface when the kernel moves under it — the next
  decade of kernel changes has almost nothing here to break.
- **one self-contained binary.** The eBPF objects are built,
  validated, and embedded at build time; at run time a deployment
  needs only a Linux kernel, root, and the single file — no
  rustup, no nightly, no bpf-linker, no daemon
  ([STABILITY.md](STABILITY.md)). The experimental half of the
  toolchain is quarantined where it cannot hurt a deployment, so
  the deployment does not inherit the toolchain's mortality.
- **mechanical reproducibility.** The prebuilt-parity gate and its
  manifest mean the embedded objects can be regenerated by a
  script, not by a person's memory of how things were done.
  Anything the project cannot re-derive, it does not really own.
- **dependency minimalism.** Every crate is a supply-chain
  commitment for the project's whole life; the audit and the
  deny.toml policy ([DEPENDENCY_AUDIT.md](DEPENDENCY_AUDIT.md))
  keep that commitment list short enough to read in one sitting.
- **the frozen CLI grammar.** Scripts written against zelynic in
  2026 still run in 2100 or the project broke its word
  ([PHILOSOPHY.md](PHILOSOPHY.md) §4). A stable interface is the
  one promise users can build decades on — it is also the
  discipline that stops complexity from sneaking in as "just one
  more flag."
- **local-first, zero telemetry.** There is no server side to keep
  alive, no account to suspend, no license-check call home. The
  tool keeps working on the day every web service of its birth era
  is gone, because it never depended on any of them.

## 3. The economics fund the second century

Sentiment does not patch kernels. The second century is paid for
the same way the first one is: by the people who profit from the
tool. The GPL-3.0-only core is free forever — that is not charity,
that is distribution insurance, the widest possible base of users
and auditors who can carry the tool forward. The commercial tiers
(Individual, Business, Company — terms and current prices in
[COMMERCIAL_LICENSE.md](COMMERCIAL_LICENSE.md), repriced as the
value compounds; the 2026-10-10 move is the recorded precedent)
are the fuel: a maintainer funded by the tiers beats a committee
funded by nobody.

License defense is part of the longevity plan, not a side quest.
Unauthorized use — stealing the code, forking it illegally,
shipping it inside a closed-source commercial product without
contacting the owner and paying — is theft, and the warning
stands in every licensing surface (README,
[COMMERCIAL_LICENSE.md](COMMERCIAL_LICENSE.md),
[LICENSING_FAQ.md](LICENSING_FAQ.md)). A project that cannot
defend its license cannot fund its maintenance; the trademark
policy ([TRADEMARK.md](../TRADEMARK.md)) carries the same logic
for the name. The prices are deliberately not hardcoded in this
doc: one authoritative surface per fact is itself a longevity
rule — fewer places to sync, fewer places to rot.

## 4. The knowledge outlives the author

A single-maintainer project dies twice: once when the maintainer
stops, and once when the understanding stops being recoverable.
The second death is the preventable one. The doc corpus is written
as a time capsule, not a diary: [PHILOSOPHY.md](PHILOSOPHY.md)
records why, [INNOVATIONS.md](INNOVATIONS.md) records what was
won and what was retired, the dated audits under
[audits/](audits/) record how every verdict was reached, and
[MAINTENANCE.md](MAINTENANCE.md) records the rituals. A competent
stranger with only this repository can reconstruct the reasoning —
which means the project's single point of failure is not a head.

The gates are the immune system that makes this stick. A rule that
lives in a maintainer's habits dies with the habit; a rule that is
machine-enforced survives owner attrition, because it fires the
same way for a successor as it did for the founder. The parity
gate, the gate battery behind `gate-keepers.sh`, the name-case
law, the permissions check, the actions-pin health check — each is
a standard that no longer depends on anyone remembering it. Where
PHILOSOPHY.md says "gates are the culture," this page says the
same thing from a century out: culture that is compiled into the
toolchain is the only culture with a shelf life longer than a
human.

## 5. The refusals

What zelynic will not do to survive is as load-bearing as what it
will do. The anti-strategy, in one list:

- No daemon creep, no telemetry, no protocol parsing, no
  cooperation from applications ([PHILOSOPHY.md](PHILOSOPHY.md)
  §1) — every one of those re-introduces a runtime dependency on
  something that can die.
- No distro-chasing beyond the kernel matrix — the kernel version
  is the contract; distributions are packaging details.
- No feature bloat. Complexity must grow slower than maintainer
  attention, or the project dies of its own success.
- No breaking the frozen CLI, ever. A century of user scripts is
  worth more than any feature.
- Growth by subtraction counts: every retirement (the retired
  innovations in [INNOVATIONS.md](INNOVATIONS.md), the dead panels
  and dead scripts removed by the hunts) is survival work, not
  housekeeping.

## 6. The century checklist

The strategy, reduced to what can be reviewed era by era. Each row
names the dimension, the mechanism that carries it, and where the
mechanism is documented:

| Dimension | Mechanism | Where |
|-----------|-----------|-------|
| Substrate | Linux + cgroup v2 + in-tree eBPF | [COSMIC_DRAGON_ARCHITECTURE.md](COSMIC_DRAGON_ARCHITECTURE.md) |
| Runtime independence | one binary, embedded prebuilt objects | [STABILITY.md](STABILITY.md) |
| Reproducibility | prebuilt-parity gate + manifest | [MAINTENANCE.md](MAINTENANCE.md) |
| Supply chain | minimal deps, deny.toml policy | [DEPENDENCY_AUDIT.md](DEPENDENCY_AUDIT.md) |
| Compatibility contract | frozen CLI + kernel matrix | [PHILOSOPHY.md](PHILOSOPHY.md), [KERNEL_COMPATIBILITY.md](KERNEL_COMPATIBILITY.md) |
| Economics | GPL core + commercial tiers | [COMMERCIAL_LICENSE.md](COMMERCIAL_LICENSE.md) |
| Defense | unauthorized-use warnings + trademark | [TRADEMARK.md](../TRADEMARK.md), [LICENSING_FAQ.md](LICENSING_FAQ.md) |
| Knowledge | the doc corpus + dated audits | [README.md](README.md) |
| Standards without memory | the machine-enforced gate battery | [MAINTENANCE.md](MAINTENANCE.md) |
| Succession | public repo, GPL-3.0-only, written rituals | [MAINTENANCE.md](MAINTENANCE.md), [../CONTRIBUTING.md](../CONTRIBUTING.md) |

And the succession terms, said plainly, because a survival doc
that flinches is worthless. If the owner stops: the repository is
public, the GPL-3.0-only license makes carrying the tool legal
for anyone who cares to, the gates encode the quality bar, and
the docs encode the reasoning. The commercial line ends with the
owner's signature unless reassigned — that is the owner's
decision, not this document's. The tool itself needs so little
that it keeps running on every host it was ever installed on,
verdict by verdict, with no one watching. That is the target
shape: not a project that fights hardest to survive, but one that
needs the least to keep being useful.
